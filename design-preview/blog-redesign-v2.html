The 2026 Skilled Worker salary threshold — what changed, who's affected, and what to do next

The £38,700 floor catches more applicants than before. Here's an honest breakdown of the new rules, the exceptions that genuinely apply, and what your options are if you fall short.

For years, the Skilled Worker visa operated on a comparatively accessible salary floor. Then April 2024 arrived. The threshold jumped from £26,200 to £38,700 in a single move — the largest single increase in the route's history. If you're applying now, renewing, or advising someone through the process, this is the number that shapes everything.

But the headline figure only tells part of the story. The thresholds interact with your occupation code, your circumstances as an applicant, and whether your employer qualifies for certain exemptions. Getting this wrong means a refused application and wasted fees. Getting it right means understanding the rules at a level of detail most guides skip over.

The new salary figures — all of them

The Home Office operates three salary tests simultaneously. Your salary must clear all three that apply to your situation. Here's how they stack up:

The going rate — the minimum salary for your specific occupation code — runs alongside these figures. If the going rate for your SOC code is higher than £38,700, that higher figure is what matters. The general threshold is a floor, not a ceiling for compliance.

The Shortage Occupation List is gone

Until April 2024, certain roles on the Shortage Occupation List qualified for a 20% salary discount. That route no longer exists. The SOL has been replaced by the Immigration Salary List — which carries no discount whatsoever.

⚠
Watch out
Older guidance and many employer HR teams still refer to "shortage occupation discounts." These no longer exist. If you were planning to use the 20% discount route, your application is based on outdated information.

The new entrant rate: who actually qualifies

The new entrant rate (£30,960) applies if you meet at least one of the following criteria:

  1. 1
    Under 26 years old at time of application
    The most straightforward qualifier. Age is assessed at the date of application, not grant. If you turn 26 during the process, the age at application date is what counts.
  2. 2
    Working towards or recently completing a UK PhD
    You must have been awarded the doctorate or be in the process of completing it. The thesis must relate to your sponsored job. This route is assessed case by case.
  3. 3
    Switched from a Student visa in the last 2 years
    If you graduated from a UK institution and are making your first switch to Skilled Worker within 24 months, you may qualify for the new entrant rate — even if you're over 26.
  4. 4
    Working in a role that qualifies under previous SOL
    Transitional protections apply for a limited window on some historical shortage occupations. This is a shrinking category — check current guidance.
The going rate catches more applicants than the general threshold. Know your SOC code before you know your salary requirement.

What counts as salary — and what doesn't

The Home Office is specific about what forms of remuneration count towards the threshold. This matters because some employers try to structure packages in ways that technically reach the number on paper but wouldn't be accepted.

ℹ
What counts
Included: Basic salary, guaranteed allowances, payments for unsocial hours that are contractually guaranteed.

Not included: Performance bonuses, overtime (unless guaranteed), benefits in kind (car allowances, healthcare, pension contributions), accommodation, meals.

If your salary falls short

This is where many guides stop. Here's what's actually available to people who don't immediately qualify:

  • Request a salary increase before applying — many sponsors will accommodate a genuine conversation framed around visa compliance
  • Check whether the new entrant rate applies to your specific circumstances
  • Consider whether a different occupation code better reflects your actual role (done carefully and honestly — misrepresentation is a serious refusal ground)
  • If you're a recent graduate, the Graduate visa buys you time to find a better-paying sponsor role
  • Partner routes may be available if your partner holds a qualifying visa — check whether your circumstances allow for dependent switching

Frequently asked questions

The threshold applies to annualised salary. If you're working part-time, your salary is pro-rated to a full-time equivalent — but the going rate is also adjusted. You cannot work part-time and then annualise to £38,700 if your actual contract pays less than the pro-rated minimum for your hours. Essentially, part-time roles need to be paid at a rate equivalent to £38,700 full-time for the going rate test to work.

Performance-related bonuses, even if consistently paid, do not count. If in doubt, ask your employer to restructure the package as guaranteed basic salary rather than bonus.
The salary on your Certificate of Sponsorship is what the Home Office assesses. If your CoS was issued at a compliant salary level, a subsequent pay rise doesn't affect the application. If your CoS was issued below threshold, you'll need your sponsor to withdraw and reissue it at the correct salary before the application can proceed.
The lower £29,000 threshold for health and care workers applies only to specific occupation codes listed in the Immigration Rules, and only when the employer is the NHS, an NHS-commissioned provider, or a social care provider regulated by the CQC. Ask your employer for the specific SOC code they're using, then verify it against the current Appendix Skilled Worker to the Immigration Rules (available on gov.uk).
