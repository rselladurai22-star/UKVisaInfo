The 2026 Skilled Worker salary threshold — what changed, who it catches, what to do next

The £38,700 floor has permanently reset expectations. Here's a plain-English breakdown of where the numbers land, which occupation codes get different treatment, and your realistic options if you fall short.

For years, the Skilled Worker visa operated on an accessible salary floor. Then April 2024 arrived. The threshold jumped from £26,200 to £38,700 in a single announcement — the largest single increase in this route's history. If you're applying now, renewing, or advising someone through the process, this is the number that shapes everything that follows.

But the headline figure only tells part of the story. The thresholds interact with your occupation code, your personal circumstances, and whether your employer qualifies for specific exemptions. Getting this wrong means a refused application and wasted fees. Getting it right means understanding the rules at a level most guides never reach.

The new salary figures — all three of them

The Home Office runs three salary tests simultaneously. Your pay must pass every test that applies to your situation. Here's how they compare:

The going rate — the minimum for your specific SOC code — sits alongside these. If the going rate for your occupation is higher than £38,700, that higher figure is what matters. The general threshold is a floor, not a compliance ceiling.

The Shortage Occupation List no longer exists

Until April 2024, roles on the Shortage Occupation List qualified for a 20% salary discount. That route is closed. The SOL has been replaced by the Immigration Salary List — which carries no discount whatsoever.

⚠
Watch out
Older guidance and many employer HR teams still refer to "shortage occupation discounts." These no longer exist. If your application plan relies on a 20% discount, it's based on outdated information.

The new entrant rate: who actually qualifies

The new entrant rate of £30,960 applies if you meet at least one of the criteria below. You don't need to meet all of them.

01
Under 26 years old at time of application
Age is assessed at the date of application, not the date of grant. If you turn 26 during processing, the date you applied is what counts.
02
Working towards or recently awarded a UK PhD
The doctorate must relate to your sponsored role. "Recently awarded" means within the last 12 months. This is assessed on the merits of the application.
03
Switching from Student visa within 2 years of graduation
If you graduated from a UK institution and are making your first switch to Skilled Worker within 24 months of your course end date, the new entrant rate may apply even if you're over 26.
The going rate catches more applicants than the general threshold. Know your SOC code before you know your salary requirement.

What counts as salary — and what doesn't

ℹ
What the Home Office accepts
Counts: Basic salary, contractually guaranteed allowances, guaranteed payments for unsocial hours written into the employment contract.

Does not count: Discretionary bonuses, overtime (unless contractually guaranteed), benefits in kind — car allowances, health insurance, pension contributions, accommodation, meals.

If your salary falls short — realistic options

  • Ask your employer for a salary increase framed around visa compliance — many sponsors respond constructively to a straightforward conversation
  • Verify whether the new entrant rate applies to your specific circumstances — students switching within 2 years are often missed
  • Check whether a different SOC code better reflects your actual role (carefully, and only if it genuinely applies)
  • Graduate visa holders: the route buys 2 years to find a better-paying sponsor without needing a CoS
  • Review whether your allowances can be restructured as guaranteed basic salary
