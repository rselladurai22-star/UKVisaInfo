The Skilled Worker visa is the UK's main work route, and in 2026 the salary bar is higher than most people expect. If your pay doesn't clear the threshold, the Home Office refuses at the salary stage — no judgement, no appeal on that ground. It takes about five minutes to check if you're safe. This is how.

01 / Three testsThe three numbers you need to know

There isn't one Skilled Worker salary threshold — there are three, and you must clear all of them at once. The Home Office takes the highest of the three as your effective bar.

04 / ISLThe Immigration Salary List — what changed in 2024

The old Shortage Occupation List was scrapped in April 2024. The Immigration Salary List (ISL) replaced it, but works slightly differently: unlike new-entrant status (which requires 70% of going rate), ISL still requires 100% of the going rate for your occupation.

ISL roles in 2026 include bricklayers and masons (5312), roofers and roof tilers (5313), construction supervisors (5330), laboratory technicians (3111), animal care services (6139, partial), stonemasons (5311), and boat and ship builders (5235).

05 / AssessmentHow the Home Office actually calculates your salary

The figure on your offer letter isn't always the figure they use. Two things catch people out more than anything else.

Does not count toward the threshold
Bonuses · commission · overtime · London weighting (in most cases) · pension contributions · accommodation allowances · in-kind benefits. Even contractual guarantees don't count unless they're written into the Certificate of Sponsorship as unconditional.
What does count
Gross basic salary. Guaranteed allowances written into the CoS and paid regardless of performance or attendance — in limited cases only.
Part-time workers — run both calculations
Your annual salary is pro-rated to 37.5 hours for the general minimum test, which can make it look fine. But the £15.88/hour test uses your actual contracted hours. A 30-hour-per-week offer at £30,000 looks like £37,500 FTE — below £41,700 — but the hourly test (£30,000 ÷ 52 ÷ 30 = £19.23) passes. Run both.

06 / ExtensionsExtensions, pay rises, and the 2023 problem

Your threshold is locked at the time the Certificate of Sponsorship is issued. If you got your visa when the threshold was lower, you're safe for the current grant — but your extension will be judged at whatever the rate is when you apply.

This is already causing real problems. People granted leave in 2022–2023 at the old £26,200 threshold will face £41,700 when they extend in 2027–2028. That's a £15,500 gap. If your employer won't match it, you'll need to move jobs before the visa expires.

07 / WorkflowHow to check your salary — step by step

Run these four checks before your employer issues the CoS. A margin of less than £500 is risky — caseworkers round down, not up.

1
Find your SOC 2020 code
Your sponsor should know. If not, the ONS SOC 2020 lookup lets you search by job title. Check the occupation description matches what you actually do — the code is everything.
2
Look up the going rate for that code
Home Office Appendix Skilled Occupations lists every SOC 2020 code with the published going rate. Your offer must meet or beat it.
3
Apply the most generous discount that fits you
New entrant, STEM PhD, or ISL — work out which applies and use the best one. New entrant is usually the most generous if you qualify.
4
Run the £15.88/hour check separately
Divide your annual salary by 52, then by your actual contracted weekly hours. Must exceed £15.88. Use your real hours, not a 37.5-hour equivalent.
