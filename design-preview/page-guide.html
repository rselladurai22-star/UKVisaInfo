The family visa £29,000 income rule — what actually counts

The minimum income requirement trips up more applications than anything else. Here's exactly which money the Home Office will and won't accept.

SRBy the UKDesk editorial team · 2 Apr 2026 · 9 min read
In short
  • You must show a gross household income of £29,000 a year to sponsor a partner.
  • Salaried employment, self-employment, pensions and some savings can all count.
  • Savings can replace income — but you need roughly £88,500 to cover the full requirement.
  • Your partner's overseas income usually doesn't count until they're in the UK.

What the requirement is

Since April 2024 the minimum income requirement for a partner (spouse or unmarried partner) visa is £29,000 a year. It replaced the old £18,600 threshold, and applies whether you apply from inside or outside the UK.

The rule exists to show you can support your partner without relying on public funds. What surprises most people is not the number — it's how narrowly the Home Office defines “income.”

The threshold isn't the hard part. Proving your income in the exact format the rules demand is.

Which income counts

Broadly, the Home Office accepts five categories. Most applicants rely on Category A — salaried employment held for at least six months with the same employer.

The savings shortcut

Savings can make up any shortfall, but the formula is steep: (£29,000 × 2.5) + £16,000 = £88,500 held for 6 months to cover the whole requirement on savings alone.

Common mistakes

The most frequent refusal reason isn't earning too little — it's evidence. Payslips that don't match bank deposits to the penny, a missing employer letter, or self-employment figures that don't line up with the SA302 will sink an otherwise strong case.
